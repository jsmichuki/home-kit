# Product Marketing Context

**Document version:** v2  
**Last updated:** 2026-09-28

## Product Overview

**One-liner:** The Complete New Homeowner System gives new homeowners the clear plan their home did not come with, so the work after closing does not become a stream of overlooked, reactive decisions.

**What it does:** The system combines eight focused PDF guides with editable companions, arranged around the sequence of homeowner tasks from closing day through the first year and later projects. Customers can buy the complete set or choose a guide for one immediate task. The resources are educational and practical; they are not property-specific inspection, legal, financial, engineering, or safety advice.

**Product category:** Digital homeowner guides, checklists, planners, and household-record organizers.

**Product type:** Direct-to-consumer digital information product.

**Business model:** One-time purchases in USD. The complete set costs $69 and includes all eight guides and companions; active individual guides cost $12–$24 each ($143 total as of 2026-09-28). Delivery starts after payment confirmation through a secure access route.

## Target Audience

**Target companies:** Not applicable; this is a B2C product.

**Decision-makers:** First-time and recent homeowners, especially people in their first year after closing. A household may use the resources together.

**Primary use case:** Resolve the uncertainty of “What should I deal with first?” after buying a home by turning many small, consequential tasks into a clear order of action.

**Jobs to be done:**
- Help me know what to do now after closing, and what can wait.
- Help me keep recurring maintenance, home records, and emergency information organized.
- Help me approach repairs, contractor hiring, and renovations with a plan and a clearer budget.

**Use cases:**
- First 24 hours, first 72 hours, and first month after closing.
- Building a monthly, seasonal, and first-year maintenance rhythm.
- Recording appliances, warranties, service history, emergency contacts, and shutoff information.
- Planning a repair reserve, comparing contractor quotes, and defining renovation scope before committing.

## Personas

Not applicable as a formal buying committee: this is a direct-to-consumer purchase. The primary user, champion, decision-maker, and financial buyer are usually the homeowner. Household partners may be co-users.

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:** Closing provides the keys but not an operating plan. A new homeowner is suddenly responsible for dozens of unfamiliar decisions—securing access, locating systems and shutoffs, documenting the home, maintaining it, storing records, and making repair decisions—without knowing what is urgent, what is important, or what can safely wait.

**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Search results, scattered articles, and generic checklists address isolated tasks rather than the connected homeowner journey.
- A single-purpose guide may solve an urgent task but does not create a reusable household system.
- Blank spreadsheets and ad hoc notes require the owner to design the process while already managing a new home.

**What it costs them:** The immediate cost is mental load and time: repeatedly searching, deciding, and reconstructing information. The practical risk is missed maintenance, difficult-to-find records during a repair or emergency, poorly prepared repair decisions, and unplanned ownership costs. Do not claim that a guide will prevent these outcomes; present them as risks of a fragmented, reactive approach.

**Emotional tension:** The buyer wants to feel like a capable homeowner, but instead may feel behind before they have even settled in—unsure, reactive, and worried that an easy-to-overlook task will become an expensive or stressful problem later.

## Problem-First Messaging Strategy

**Positioning principle:** Sell the felt problem and the desired state before introducing the product, its contents, price, or bundle savings. The offer should feel like the logical answer to a problem the visitor has already recognized—not a collection of files asking to be justified.

**Core reframe:** The problem is not that a homeowner lacks more information. It is that the information and tasks have no dependable order. The product is the practical system that creates that order.

**Primary message:** “Your home did not come with an owner’s manual. This is it.”

**Message sequence:**
1. **Name the moment:** After closing, the owner has the keys and a long list of unfamiliar responsibilities.
2. **Make the problem concrete:** Show the competing decisions: access, shutoffs, safety, baseline records, maintenance, warranties, repairs, budgets, and contractors.
3. **Surface the cost:** A reactive, scattered approach consumes attention now and can leave records, maintenance, and major decisions poorly prepared later.
4. **Explain why the status quo fails:** Searching as problems arise and collecting isolated checklists does not create a household system or establish priorities.
5. **Offer the new belief:** A calmer first year begins with knowing what matters now and what can wait.
6. **Introduce the solution:** The Complete New Homeowner System is the owner’s manual and editable working system for that order of operations.
7. **Prove the answer:** Show the eight guides, their companions, sample practical pages, delivery clarity, and the $69 complete-set value.
8. **Prompt action:** Invite the visitor to get the complete system while the motivation to create order is present.

**Page and campaign guardrails:**
- Lead with the homeowner’s situation, not “8 guides,” “editable companions,” or the $69 price.
- Use concrete household moments rather than vague anxiety: standing in a new kitchen, not knowing the water shutoff, finding an appliance manual too late, or preparing for a repair without a record or plan.
- Build contrast between the “react when something breaks” path and the “know what to do next” path without fearmongering or invented consequences.
- Introduce the complete set only after the visitor understands why isolated answers are insufficient.
- Use bundle price and $143 individual-total comparison as rational reinforcement after the problem and solution fit are clear, not as the opening hook.
- Keep the solution credible: it organizes work and supports better preparation; it does not promise to prevent emergencies, save a stated amount, or replace professionals.

## Competitive Landscape

**Direct:** No named direct competitors or competitor research are documented in this repository. Validate named alternatives before making comparative claims.

**Secondary:** Standalone homeowner checklists, maintenance planners, and single-topic digital guides — useful for one task, but not designed as a connected, eight-part homeowner reference library.

**Indirect:** DIY web searches, notes apps, blank spreadsheets, paper folders, and relying on memory — familiar and low-cost, but fragmented and dependent on the homeowner assembling and maintaining the system alone.

## Differentiation

**Key differentiators:**
- Eight guides follow the homeowner journey: set up, maintain, protect, budget, and improve.
- Each guide includes an editable companion, such as a checklist, tracker, worksheet, or workbook.
- The full library is available as one complete system for $69, versus $143 when the current guides are bought separately.
- The product is designed as a useful kitchen-table reference, rather than generic reading material.

**How we do it differently:** Rather than selling eight unrelated downloads, the product first addresses the homeowner’s lack of a plan, then organizes the answer into focused modules that work individually for an urgent need and collectively as an owner’s manual for the home.

**Why that's better:** Owners can start with the task in front of them while keeping related guidance, records, and planning tools ready for later moments.

**Why customers choose us:** A calm, practical, editable system that turns the uncertainty after closing into a clear starting point and a reusable structure for ongoing ownership.

## Objections

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| “I only need help with one task right now.” | Individual guides are available; the complete set is positioned for owners who want the full reference library ready as needs change. |
| “Will I receive physical books or a specific kind of professional advice?” | These are digital PDF guides with editable companions, delivered after payment confirmation. They are educational resources, not property-specific professional advice. |
| “What if I cannot access my files or they are not what I ordered?” | The product has a secure delivery route and support channel. The current draft refund policy covers unavailable, materially different, or defective files; finalize policy language before making stronger promises. |

**Anti-persona:** People seeking a property inspection, personalized legal/financial/engineering/safety advice, commercial redistribution rights, or a purely inspirational home-design product rather than practical planning resources.

## Switching Dynamics

*The following Four Forces are positioning hypotheses inferred from the product and should be validated with customer interviews.*

**Push:** The owner keeps encountering important tasks without a clear priority, and the do-it-as-needed approach leaves them feeling reactive, scattered, and unsure whether they have overlooked something consequential.

**Pull:** One affordable, editable guide system that says what matters now, supports the first year, and remains useful for repairs and projects.

**Habit:** Searching only when something breaks, keeping information in multiple places, or postponing organization because there is always a more urgent household task.

**Anxiety:** Concern that a digital guide will be generic, not fit the home or location, duplicate free information online, or be difficult to access after purchase.
