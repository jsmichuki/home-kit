## Pages 2–3 — The maintenance mindset

Maintenance is the small, repeatable work that helps a house last longer and gives you more notice before a repair becomes urgent. It does not mean you must fix everything yourself.

### Preventive versus reactive care

| Preventive care | Reactive repair |
|---|---|
| Inspecting a supply line for moisture | Repairing water damage after a leak |
| Clearing a downspout so water drains away | Addressing water that entered a basement |
| Changing a filter when needed | Calling for service after airflow drops |
| Recording a strange sound early | Replacing a failed component without history |

**Your simple system:** notice → record → decide → schedule → save the result.

### Four rules that keep the system manageable

1. **Start with a baseline.** Use the inspection report, manuals, and your first walkthrough.
2. **Follow the specific manual.** Manufacturer guidance wins over a generic checklist.
3. **Make tasks seasonal.** Pair work with weather and routines already on your calendar.
4. **Call help early for safety or uncertainty.** The win is informed action, not DIY bravado.

## Pages 4–6 — Monthly home reset

### Once a month: a 20-minute visual sweep

- [ ] Test smoke and carbon monoxide alarms using their test buttons; address low-battery or replacement alerts.
- [ ] Check the HVAC filter according to its condition and the manufacturer’s guidance.
- [ ] Look under sinks, around toilets, and behind accessible appliances for moisture.
- [ ] Notice drains: repeated slow draining, gurgling, or odor deserves attention.
- [ ] Check refrigerator/freezer temperature and door seals; clean visible spills.
- [ ] Walk the outside from the ground: note loose items, damaged screens, standing water, or visible changes.
- [ ] Look for new stains on ceilings/walls, unusual odors, pest signs, or changes in humidity.
- [ ] Add completed work, costs, and observations to your record.

## Pages 7–9 — Quarterly checks

Do these four times a year, or assign one group to the start of each new season.

### Water and plumbing

- [ ] Inspect exposed supply lines, shutoffs, and appliance hoses for corrosion or moisture.
- [ ] Check toilets for persistent running or water at the base.
- [ ] Confirm water flows away from the foundation and downspouts discharge appropriately.

### Heating, cooling, and ventilation

- [ ] Review filter condition and airflow.
- [ ] Keep outdoor HVAC equipment clear of leaves, clutter, and vegetation as manufacturer guidance permits.
- [ ] Confirm bath and kitchen exhaust fans move air effectively.

### Appliances and electrical

- [ ] Check washer hoses and dishwasher connections where accessible.
- [ ] Clean refrigerator coils or filters only as the appliance manual recommends.
- [ ] Test labeled GFCI outlets; do not force a breaker or outlet that will not reset.

### Exterior

- [ ] From the ground, scan roof edges, siding, flashing, gutters, and foundation for visible changes.
- [ ] Check doors, windows, screens, weatherstripping, and exterior caulk.
- [ ] Trim vegetation away from the home where appropriate; keep vents unobstructed.

## Pages 10–11 — Semiannual maintenance

### Twice a year, set aside a deeper check

- [ ] Arrange gutter/downspout cleaning or inspect from a safe position.
- [ ] Visually inspect roof condition from the ground; arrange professional inspection if damage is visible or suspected.
- [ ] Review window and door seals; clean tracks and address worn weatherstripping.
- [ ] Review water-heater area for leaks, corrosion, unusual noise, and clear access. Follow the manual for any maintenance.
- [ ] Schedule heating/cooling service as appropriate to your equipment and climate.
- [ ] Review fire extinguishers, alarms, emergency supplies, and household escape plan.
- [ ] Inspect accessible attic, crawl-space, basement, or utility areas for moisture, pests, odors, or insulation concerns—without entering unsafe spaces.

## Pages 12–16 — Annual maintenance by system

### Roof, drainage, and exterior

- [ ] Obtain a professional roof evaluation when its condition, age, storm exposure, or visible damage warrants it.
- [ ] Confirm gutters, downspouts, grading, and drains route water away from the home.
- [ ] Inspect exterior paint, siding, masonry, decks, rails, and steps for deterioration.

### Foundation and structure

- [ ] Photograph and date any cracks or changes you are monitoring.
- [ ] Watch for persistent moisture, sticking doors, new gaps, or movement; seek a qualified evaluation when changes are new, widening, or concerning.

### Plumbing

- [ ] Review the main shutoff and fixture shutoffs; ensure household members know the main location.
- [ ] Follow manufacturer/service guidance for water heater and water treatment equipment.
- [ ] Plan septic or well service according to local conditions and qualified guidance if applicable.

### Electrical and safety

- [ ] Review panel labels and keep access clear.
- [ ] Replace smoke/CO alarms and extinguishers according to dates and manufacturer guidance.
- [ ] Have recurring breaker trips, buzzing, warmth, damaged outlets, or flickering assessed by a qualified electrician.

### Heating, cooling, appliances, garage, and yard

- [ ] Schedule service appropriate to HVAC equipment and warranty requirements.
- [ ] Review appliance manuals, filters, hoses, and vents.
- [ ] Test garage-door safety reverse function; lubricate only as the manufacturer recommends.
- [ ] Review trees, irrigation, fences, outdoor lighting, and drainage before they create risk or damage.

## Pages 17–26 — Area-by-area checklists

### Kitchen

- [ ] Look under sink and around dishwasher for moisture.
- [ ] Check refrigerator seals, water line/filter as applicable, and ventilation clearances.
- [ ] Clean range-hood filter and verify exhaust path per manual.
- [ ] Note cabinet swelling, loose caulk, or repeated drain issues.

### Bathrooms

- [ ] Test exhaust fan and check for moisture or mildew.
- [ ] Inspect caulk/grout and the area below fixtures.
- [ ] Check toilet stability, running water, and slow drainage.

### HVAC and ventilation

- [ ] Record filter size and replacement date.
- [ ] Keep registers, returns, and equipment access clear.
- [ ] Note new noises, weak airflow, short cycling, or inconsistent temperatures.

### Plumbing

- [ ] Know the main shutoff location.
- [ ] Look for moisture around fixtures, exposed pipes, and water heater.
- [ ] Protect pipes from freezing conditions as locally appropriate.

### Electrical

- [ ] Keep panel area accessible and labels legible.
- [ ] Test labeled GFCIs.
- [ ] Record recurring trips, flickers, warm outlets, or damaged cords—do not ignore them.

### Roof, attic, basement, and crawl space

- [ ] Look for water stains, damp smells, pest activity, daylight where it should not be, or new insulation issues.
- [ ] Observe from a safe location; do not enter cramped, hot, wet, or unsafe areas.

### Garage, yard, and exterior

- [ ] Check door movement, safety sensors, rails, exterior lighting, and weather seals.
- [ ] Keep soil and mulch from contacting vulnerable siding; keep drainage paths clear.
- [ ] Watch mature trees and branches near rooflines or power lines; use qualified help where needed.

## Pages 27–29 — Warning signs not to ignore

These signs do not diagnose a cause. They tell you it is wise to document the issue and consider timely professional assessment.

| What you notice | First response |
|---|---|
| New water stain, active drip, or musty odor | Photograph it, limit further water exposure if safe, and seek help for active or recurring moisture. |
| Repeated breaker trips, buzzing, hot outlet, or burning smell | Stop using the affected area if safe; seek qualified electrical help promptly. |
| Gas odor or suspected gas leak | Leave the area, avoid switches/flames, and follow your utility’s emergency instructions. |
| Persistent slow drains, gurgling, or sewage odor | Avoid harsh improvised fixes; record affected fixtures and contact a qualified plumber if persistent. |
| New/widening crack, movement, or doors suddenly sticking | Photograph and date the change; arrange a qualified evaluation if it is new or concerning. |
| HVAC noise, poor airflow, unusual cycling, or water around equipment | Check the filter/access area only if safe; arrange service rather than disassembling equipment. |
| Missing roof material, interior stain, or storm damage | Document from the ground; avoid roof access and seek professional/insurance guidance as appropriate. |

### The “done is better than ideal” pledge

Choose one recurring day, one place to keep records, and one method to schedule reminders. A small routine you repeat is more valuable than a perfect plan you never open.
