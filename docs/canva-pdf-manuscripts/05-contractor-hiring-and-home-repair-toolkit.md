## Pages 7–10 — Questions to ask a contractor

### Experience and approach

- Have you completed comparable work? May I see relevant examples or references?
- What do you think is happening, and what alternatives should I understand?
- Who will be on site, and who is my day-to-day contact?

### Qualifications and documentation

- Are you properly licensed where this work requires it?
- Can you provide current insurance information and references?
- Who is responsible for permits, inspections, and applicable approvals?
- Will subcontractors be used, and how are they managed?

### Scope, schedule, and materials

- What exactly is included and excluded from the written scope?
- Which materials, allowances, brands, or equivalents are assumed?
- What is the expected start date, duration, and schedule risk?
- How will site protection, cleanup, and disposal be handled?

### Payment, changes, and warranty

- What is the payment schedule tied to?
- How will a change in scope, price, or schedule be approved?
- What workmanship or product warranties apply, and how are they documented?

Record answers; do not rely on a verbal memory.

## Pages 14–15 — Red flags to slow down for

- Pressure to make an immediate decision or pay an unusual amount upfront.
- A vague scope, verbal promises, or important terms that will not be put in writing.
- Missing or inconsistent identity, insurance, licensing, reference, or contact information.
- A payment schedule disconnected from meaningful progress.
- Unclear responsibility for permits, subcontractors, materials, cleanup, or changes.
- A quote dramatically lower than the others without a clear explanation.

These signs are prompts to ask more questions, verify information, or seek another option—not a substitute for local consumer-protection guidance.
