## Page 2 — How to use this binder

Complete it when things are calm. Keep a printed copy where household members can find it and a secure digital copy that is accessible when away from home. Review it after a move, a major repair, or once each year.

> **Emergency reminder:** This binder supports a plan; it does not replace emergency services, utility instructions, or professional advice. In a life-threatening emergency, call local emergency services.

## Pages 5–7 — Water emergency plan

Water moves quickly. Every household member who can safely do so should know the main shutoff location.

### Fill this in

- **Main water shutoff location:** ______________________________
- **How to access it:** _______________________________________
- **Fixture shutoffs I know:** kitchen / toilets / laundry / other: __________
- **Plumber or emergency contact:** ___________________________

### If you find active water

1. Protect people first—avoid areas with possible electrical danger.
2. If safe and you know the correct control, stop the water at the nearest practical shutoff.
3. Move valuables only if it is safe to do so; photograph the condition.
4. Contact appropriate help: plumber, restoration company, insurer, or emergency service.
5. Do not assume wet materials are harmless just because the flow stopped.

## Pages 11–13 — Fire preparedness and home escape plan

## Pages 14–15 — Severe weather preparation

Local risks differ. Use official local alerts and adapt this framework to your climate, building type, and household.

### Before a forecasted event

- [ ] Charge phones and backup batteries.
- [ ] Review alerts, evacuation routes, and local guidance.
- [ ] Bring loose outdoor items inside or secure them if safe.
- [ ] Check essentials: water, medications, pet supplies, lighting, and key documents.
- [ ] Park and fuel vehicles according to local conditions.
- [ ] Photograph the home exterior and major possessions if time permits.

### After conditions are safe

- [ ] Check for hazards before entering damaged areas.
- [ ] Document damage with photos and notes.
- [ ] Avoid downed lines, floodwater, and unstable structures.
- [ ] Follow utility, insurer, and local-authority instructions.

## Pages 18–19 — Evacuation and emergency documents

### Grab-and-go checklist

- [ ] Phone, keys, wallet, essential medication
- [ ] Household/pet needs
- [ ] Printed emergency contacts
- [ ] Insurance and identification information
- [ ] Backup charger / flashlight if readily available
- [ ] Important documents or secure digital access

### Documents to keep accessible

- Identification, insurance policy, and claims contacts
- Mortgage/lease and property information
- Emergency contacts and medical/pet care information
- Photos or inventory of major possessions
- Critical appliance, utility, and service records

Do not delay evacuation to gather paperwork.
