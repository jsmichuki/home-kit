## Page 2 — Welcome: Start with the essentials

You do not need to understand every part of your house today. Your first job is simpler: make it safe, make it secure, and learn where the important systems are.

Use this guide in order when you can. If a task does not apply to your home, mark it **N/A** and move on. Take photos as you go—they are often the fastest way to build a useful home record.

**Your first-month priorities**

1. **Secure access** — locks, codes, accounts, and keys.
2. **Find the shutoffs** — water, electricity, gas (if present), and internet equipment.
3. **Check life safety** — smoke alarms, carbon monoxide alarms, exits, and extinguishers.
4. **Create a baseline** — photos, meter readings, documents, and a list of questions.
5. **Catch small problems early** — leaks, drainage issues, appliance concerns, and unfinished repair items.

> **A useful rule:** If you discover active water, a gas odor, sparking, smoke, or a condition that feels unsafe, stop the checklist and seek appropriate emergency or professional help.

## Pages 3–4 — First 24 hours

**[IMAGE DIRECTION]** Clean top-down flat lay: labeled house key, flashlight, smartphone camera, small notebook, outlet tester, and a simple home floor-plan card. Keep it aspirational and uncluttered.

### First 24 Hours Checklist

#### Make the home safe to occupy

- [ ] Confirm electricity, water, heating/cooling, and refrigerator/freezer are operating.
- [ ] Walk every exterior door and accessible window. Check that it closes, latches, and locks.
- [ ] Locate the main water shutoff and photograph it.
- [ ] Locate the electrical panel and photograph it with the door open.
- [ ] If your home has gas, identify the meter/shutoff area and save the utility emergency number. Do not operate utility equipment unless you know it is safe and permitted.
- [ ] Test smoke alarms and carbon monoxide alarms using their test buttons. Note any missing, expired, or nonworking alarms.
- [ ] Confirm your fire extinguisher is present, accessible, and in date if applicable.

#### Record the condition you received

- [ ] Photograph each room, including floors, walls, ceilings, and closets.
- [ ] Photograph meter readings where applicable.
- [ ] Photograph existing scratches, stains, cracks, or unfinished items.
- [ ] Save inspection reports, closing documents, manuals, and seller-provided records in one folder.
- [ ] Start a “Questions / Follow-up” list—do not rely on memory during a move.

#### Check the basics

- [ ] Open the refrigerator and freezer after a few hours; confirm both are cooling.
- [ ] Run water briefly at each fixture and look below sinks for drips.
- [ ] Flush each toilet and watch for leaks at the base or supply line.
- [ ] Note unusual odors, noises, or moisture.

## Pages 5–6 — First 72 hours: take control of access

### Secure every way into the home

- [ ] Rekey or replace exterior door locks, or arrange for a qualified locksmith.
- [ ] Change garage-door opener codes and remove old remotes from the system if possible.
- [ ] Change alarm, camera, doorbell, and smart-lock accounts; remove prior owners’ access.
- [ ] Change Wi-Fi network name and password; update router administrator credentials.
- [ ] Test garage safety reversal and the door from inside the home.
- [ ] Identify spare keys and choose one safe, intentional backup arrangement.
- [ ] Review the inspection report and list items that are urgent, time-sensitive, or worth monitoring.

### Sort discoveries by urgency

| Act now | Schedule soon | Watch and record |
|---|---|---|
| Active leak, electrical hazard, no heat/cooling in extreme conditions, insecure entry, gas odor | Dripping fixture, damaged caulk, aging filter, loose handrail, slow drain | Hairline cosmetic crack, minor scuff, intermittent noise, settling nail pop |

*When you are unsure, get a qualified professional’s assessment rather than guessing at the cause.*

### Home setup checklist

#### Accounts and services

- [ ] Confirm utility accounts and billing start dates.
- [ ] Set up internet service and save equipment details.
- [ ] Learn garbage, recycling, and yard-waste schedules.
- [ ] Complete mail forwarding and update your primary address where needed.
- [ ] Save mortgage-servicer login and payment details in a secure place.
- [ ] Save insurer and policy information; confirm how to report a claim.
- [ ] Read applicable HOA/condo rules, parking guidance, trash rules, and approval processes.

#### People and places

- [ ] Add key local contacts: utility emergency lines, non-emergency services, plumber, electrician, HVAC provider, locksmith, and restoration company.
- [ ] Introduce yourself to a nearby neighbor if comfortable; they may know local routines and shared conditions.
- [ ] Identify the nearest urgent care, pharmacy, hardware store, and emergency meeting point.

## Pages 9–10 — Safety walkthrough, room by room

### Entry, halls, and stairs

- [ ] Keep exits clear and confirm deadbolts open easily from the inside.
- [ ] Check railings, stair treads, and lighting.
- [ ] Place a flashlight where you can reach it during a power outage.

### Kitchen and laundry

- [ ] Know where the water supply lines are and look for drips.
- [ ] Ensure major appliances are stable and vents are not blocked.
- [ ] Confirm the dryer exhausts outdoors and plan a cleaning if its service history is unknown.
- [ ] Keep combustibles away from cooking surfaces and dryer equipment.

### Bathrooms

- [ ] Test exhaust fans, then check for mildew or peeling paint nearby.
- [ ] Check caulk and grout around wet areas; note gaps or soft surfaces.
- [ ] Identify GFCI-protected outlets and test according to their labels.

### Bedrooms and living areas

- [ ] Confirm smoke/CO alarms are present and functional where required.
- [ ] Open windows that serve as emergency exits; make sure they are not painted shut or blocked.
- [ ] Check accessible outlets, cords, and covers for visible damage.

### Outside and utility areas

- [ ] Note drainage paths: water should move away from the foundation.
- [ ] Look for loose steps, handrails, branches, or trip hazards.
- [ ] Keep electrical panels, water shutoffs, and HVAC access clear.

## Pages 14–15 — Create your home records system

The goal is not perfect paperwork. It is being able to find the right thing quickly: a warranty, a paint color, an insurance policy, or the name of the person who serviced the furnace.

### Save these first

- Closing, inspection, appraisal, and survey documents
- Insurance policy and agent/claims contacts
- Mortgage, tax, HOA, and utility information
- Appliance manuals, model/serial numbers, receipts, and warranties
- Before-and-after photos of repairs or renovations
- Contractor quotes, invoices, permits, and final sign-offs

### A simple digital folder structure

```text
My Home/
├── 01 Purchase & Closing/
├── 02 Insurance & Mortgage/
├── 03 Inspection & Property Info/
├── 04 Appliances & Manuals/
├── 05 Repairs & Maintenance/
├── 06 Contractors & Renovations/
├── 07 Warranties & Receipts/
└── 08 Home Inventory/
```

> **Make it easy:** take a photo of every paper receipt or invoice before filing it. Use a consistent file name, such as `2026-03-HVAC-service-company.pdf`.

## Pages 16–17 — First-month maintenance sweep

This is a visual, non-invasive check—not a request to take apart unfamiliar equipment.

- [ ] Check HVAC filter size and condition; replace it if needed and label the date.
- [ ] Arrange dryer-vent cleaning if it is overdue or its history is unknown.
- [ ] From the ground, visually inspect gutters, downspouts, roof edges, and drainage.
- [ ] Look under all sinks, behind accessible appliances, and around toilets for moisture.
- [ ] Inspect bathtub, shower, and sink caulk for gaps or peeling.
- [ ] Test GFCI outlets with their built-in test/reset buttons where labeled.
- [ ] Check visible plumbing for corrosion, moisture, or loose connections.
- [ ] Walk the perimeter after rain if possible; note standing water or flow toward the home.
- [ ] Clean range hood filters and check accessible appliance vents.
- [ ] Record every completed task in your maintenance log.

## Pages 18–19 — If the home was previously occupied

Some of the most useful questions are not obvious during a showing. Work through this list while seller details, listing photos, and inspection records are easy to find.

- [ ] Which systems were recently repaired or replaced? Save the paperwork.
- [ ] Are there transferable warranties, service plans, or manuals?
- [ ] Which paint colors, flooring materials, or fixtures will be hard to match later?
- [ ] Where are irrigation controls, outdoor shutoffs, cleanouts, attic access, and appliance shutoffs?
- [ ] Are there recurring quirks: a tripped breaker, a slow drain, a sticky door, seasonal water, or a temperamental appliance?
- [ ] Which items are owned, leased, rented, or monitored by a third party?
- [ ] Are there codes, fobs, keys, remotes, or smart-home accounts still to transfer?
