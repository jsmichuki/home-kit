## Pages 4–6 — Define the scope

Scope is the boundary of the project: what will change, what will not, and what decisions still need to be made.

### Reality-check questions

- Is any part of the project likely to affect structure, water, electrical, gas, ventilation, or accessibility?
- Are permits, HOA/condo approvals, or inspections potentially applicable?
- Will other work need to happen first?
- Where will household members, pets, furniture, and deliveries go during the work?

Check applicable local requirements and use qualified professionals for work beyond your expertise.

### Contingency note

A contingency is a planned response to uncertainty, not permission to spend without a decision. Keep it visible. If you use it, record why.

## Pages 18–20 — Project timeline

### Typical project phases

1. Define the project and budget range
2. Measure, research, and gather quotes
3. Finalize scope, approvals, and selections
4. Order materials; confirm lead times
5. Prepare the home and protect adjacent areas
6. Demolition / installation / inspections as applicable
7. Punch list, completion, records, and warranty storage
