# A. Spring Home Care Guide

> **Refresh, inspect, and direct water away from the home.**

## Design brief

**Palette:** fresh sage, soft sky blue, warm white.  
**[IMAGE DIRECTION — COVER]** Home exterior after gentle rain, fresh green planting, homeowner checking a downspout from the ground; soft spring light, no dramatic weather.

## Page 2 — Spring priorities

Spring is a good time to look for the effects of winter weather and prepare the home for warmer, wetter months. Begin with drainage and visible exterior changes.

### Your spring focus

1. Move water away from the home.
2. Check roof edges, gutters, exterior surfaces, and screens from safe locations.
3. Prepare cooling and outdoor systems as appropriate.
4. Record what changed over winter.

## Pages 3–5 — Spring checklist

### Exterior, roof, and drainage

- [ ] Walk the property after rain if possible; note standing water or water moving toward the foundation.
- [ ] Check gutters/downspouts from a safe position; arrange cleaning or repair when needed.
- [ ] From the ground, inspect roof edges, siding, trim, steps, rails, and screens for winter damage.
- [ ] Check that soil, mulch, and vegetation are not trapping moisture against vulnerable exterior surfaces.

### Cooling, windows, and plumbing

- [ ] Review HVAC filter and schedule cooling service where appropriate.
- [ ] Clean window tracks; inspect screens and weatherstripping.
- [ ] Check accessible outdoor faucets, hose connections, and indoor plumbing for leaks after freeze season.
- [ ] Test bath/kitchen exhaust fans and note any moisture or mildew.

### Yard and cleaning

- [ ] Inspect trees and branches near structures or power lines; use qualified help for hazardous work.
- [ ] Check irrigation only when conditions allow; correct visible leaks or overspray.
- [ ] Clean exterior entries and clear drains, grates, and walkways.

# B. Summer Home Care Guide

> **Protect comfort, manage heat and moisture, and keep outdoor areas working.**

## Design brief

**Palette:** sun-washed sand, deep teal, white.  
**[IMAGE DIRECTION — COVER]** Bright, shaded home patio with healthy landscaping and a homeowner checking an outdoor HVAC unit from a respectful distance; editorial and calm.

## Page 2 — Summer priorities

Summer puts pressure on cooling, water use, exterior finishes, pests, and outdoor living areas. A short routine can make hot-weather problems easier to spot.

## Pages 3–5 — Summer checklist

### Cooling and indoor comfort

- [ ] Check HVAC filter and note rooms with uneven comfort or unusual system noise.
- [ ] Keep registers, returns, and outdoor equipment access clear.
- [ ] Use bath/kitchen ventilation to manage indoor moisture.
- [ ] Check refrigerator/freezer seals and temperatures during hot spells.

### Exterior and water

- [ ] Watch irrigation for leaks, overspray, or water running toward the home.
- [ ] Inspect decks, patios, railings, fences, and outdoor furniture for loose or weathered areas.
- [ ] Check caulk, paint, and exterior seals for sun or storm wear.
- [ ] Clear leaves and debris around outdoor equipment and drainage points.

### Pests and storms

- [ ] Look for nests, insect activity, chewed material, or gaps at exterior penetrations.
- [ ] Secure or store loose outdoor items before severe-weather conditions.
- [ ] Review local storm alerts and household emergency supplies.

# C. Fall Home Care Guide

> **Prepare the home for cooler weather, falling leaves, and winter conditions.**

## Design brief

**Palette:** rust, forest green, parchment.  
**[IMAGE DIRECTION — COVER]** Warm fall exterior scene with leaves, a tidy front door, and homeowner safely observing gutters from ground level; elevated but approachable.

## Page 2 — Fall priorities

Fall is the season to prepare systems for heating, manage leaves and water, and close small gaps before colder weather makes them more consequential.

## Pages 3–5 — Fall checklist

### Heating and indoor safety

- [ ] Review HVAC filter and arrange heating service where appropriate.
- [ ] Test smoke/CO alarms; replace batteries or equipment according to manufacturer guidance.
- [ ] Keep heating equipment access clear and follow appliance manual guidance.
- [ ] Check fireplaces, chimneys, or solid-fuel appliances with qualified service when applicable.

### Gutters, roof, and seals

- [ ] Clear leaves from drainage routes; arrange safe gutter service as needed.
- [ ] Observe roof edges, flashing, and exterior from the ground.
- [ ] Check door sweeps, weatherstripping, and window seals.
- [ ] Inspect exterior caulk and repair appropriate gaps before wet/cold weather.

### Plumbing and outdoor equipment

- [ ] Adapt outdoor faucet, irrigation, hose, and pool/spa care to local freeze guidance.
- [ ] Store or protect seasonal outdoor equipment.
- [ ] Note trees/branches that may pose seasonal risk and plan qualified service when needed.

# D. Winter Home Care Guide

> **Stay warm, manage moisture, and prepare for weather-related disruption.**

## Design brief

**Palette:** deep navy, frosted blue, warm cream.  
**[IMAGE DIRECTION — COVER]** Cozy, warmly lit home interior on a winter day; subtle view of weather outside, a blanket and mug, no blizzard drama, title space in upper third.

## Page 2 — Winter priorities

Winter routines differ dramatically. In freeze-prone areas, pipe protection and snow/ice considerations may be central. In milder climates, rain, wind, indoor moisture, and heating performance can matter more.

## Pages 3–5 — Winter checklist

### Heating and indoor moisture

- [ ] Check HVAC filter and note uneven heat, unusual noise, or repeated cycling.
- [ ] Keep vents, returns, and equipment access clear.
- [ ] Use exhaust fans and ventilation to manage moisture; watch for persistent condensation or mildew.
- [ ] Test smoke/CO alarms and keep emergency lighting available.

### Freeze and weather protection where applicable

- [ ] Follow local/manufacturer guidance for protecting exposed or vulnerable pipes.
- [ ] Know where the main water shutoff is before a freeze event.
- [ ] Disconnect or protect outdoor hoses/faucets as appropriate to your climate.
- [ ] Keep safe indoor temperatures during cold spells; seek qualified help for suspected frozen pipes or loss of heat.

### Snow, ice, rain, and power interruptions

- [ ] Follow local guidance for snow/ice removal; avoid unsafe roof work.
- [ ] Keep paths and entries clear enough to reduce slip risk.
- [ ] Check flashlights, chargers, medications, water, pet supplies, and emergency contacts.
- [ ] After severe weather, inspect from safe locations and document visible damage.

# Shared final page — Seasonal annual loop

**Spring:** water and exterior reset  
**Summer:** cooling, moisture, outdoor care  
**Fall:** heat, leaves, sealing  
**Winter:** warmth, moisture, emergency readiness

### Keep one small habit

At the start of each season, choose three tasks, take a short exterior photo set, and write down one observation. Your future maintenance decisions will be better because you noticed the pattern.
